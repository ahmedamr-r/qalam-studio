## What to find out

Look only for what bears on the problem they're working out. Leave the rest alone, however you'd design it.

From the customer's side, on each screen:

- Its one job: what someone came to this screen to do, and whether that's the first thing they see.
- What's in the way of doing it: a step that could go, a field nobody needs, a control that doesn't look like what it does, two actions competing for the same press.
- What's unclear at the moment of deciding: a total, a fee, a time, a condition, a word only the team uses.
- What's missing at that moment: what someone needs to see to go on, such as the price with everything in it, when it arrives, what happens next, or how to undo it.
- What pulls them away: a box that sends them off to look for something, a link out, a surprise.
- Whether they can read it and reach it: contrast, size, a target too small, words cut off. Only where it bears on the problem.

From the business's side:

- What the screen earns from, and what it gives away: a discount shown where nobody needed it, a threshold almost nobody reaches.
- What it costs when it goes wrong: people writing in, refunds, payments that fail.
- Which number it moves, and how this screen moves it.

Across the screens, in the order they were shown:

- Where something first appears that someone needed a screen earlier.
- A step that repeats another, or could go.

## What people usually miss

- Fixing by adding: another banner, another line of help, another badge, when something already on the screen is in the way.
- Several fixes on one screen at once: nobody can tell which one helped, and the screen gets louder each time.
- Breaking what works: changing the part people use without trouble.
- A cause that isn't on the screen at all: the price itself, a fee, a delivery time, stock. No layout fixes it; say so instead.
- The screen before: where people leave is where it shows, not always where it starts.
- Judging the screen by taste instead of by the problem.

## How to ask

How to point at what you found and word it:

- One finding per thing, worded as what goes wrong and for whom, in plain words.
- Point at it by the words printed on the screen, exactly as they're printed, so it can be marked where it is. Where it has no words (a picture, a colour, a gap), say where on the screen it is.
- Say in a sentence how it bears on the problem they're working out.
- A finding from the business's side rests on how their business works, a goal or what they said: name which.
- Rank them by what each costs the problem, the worst first, and leave out what doesn't bear on it.
- Never quote a word or a number that isn't printed on the screens or in what they said.

## What to propose

- One change for each finding: the smallest that would fix it.
- Swap, don't add: a change that puts something on a screen names what it replaces or takes away there. When nothing can go, propose another change, or none.
- Prefer taking away, rewording or moving over adding.
- Never a redesign or a new screen, and never two changes on the same thing.
- Where the fix isn't on the screen (the price itself), say so and propose no change there.
