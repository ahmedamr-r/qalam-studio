# Where the playbooks come from

The playbooks in these folders are Qalam's own words. Each one names, in its front matter, the work it learned from. Nothing here is sent to Qalam AI; this file and the `credits` fields are for people reading the code.

What they draw on, and under what terms:

- **anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins** (Apache-2.0), the product-management plugin's `product-brainstorming` skill: exploring a problem before solving it (who has it, what they do today, a symptom told apart from its cause). https://github.com/anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins
- **phuryn/pm-skills** (MIT): `business-model`, `lean-canvas`, `startup-canvas`, `north-star-metric` and `identify-assumptions-existing`, for which parts a business covers and what can go wrong with a change to something that exists. https://github.com/phuryn/pm-skills
- **mattpocock/skills** (MIT): `grilling` (ask what can be decided now, find facts instead of asking for them), `wayfinder` (a map of open decisions) and `teach` (meeting someone at their level). Qalam's interview (src/asking/) already follows `grilling`. https://github.com/mattpocock/skills
- Ideas only, no text: Y Combinator Startup School's "How to get and evaluate startup ideas" and "How to talk to users", and its application's questions; Strategyzer's Business Model Canvas and Value Proposition Canvas; Ash Maurya's Lean Canvas; Rob Fitzpatrick's The Mom Test.

The skill in `src/consult/skills/screens.md` (what Qalam AI goes by when it looks at screenshots for a problem) draws on:

- **anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins** (Apache-2.0), the design plugin's `design-critique` skill: whether a screen's purpose is clear, whether people reach their goal, steps that could go, hierarchy, reading and reaching it, and a narrower look when there's a focus.
- **coreyhaines31/marketingskills** (MIT), its `cro` skill: the one action a screen is for, objections such as price and risk answered where people decide, friction, the worst first. https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills
- **Owl-Listener/designer-skills** (MIT), its heuristic evaluation and visual critique: controls that look like what they do, a screen overloaded or too sparse, one fix for each issue. https://github.com/Owl-Listener/designer-skills
- Ideas only, no text: Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics; the W3C's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines.

Not used: Dean Peters' Product-Manager-Skills, which is licensed for non-commercial use only (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

The MIT and Apache-2.0 notices of the projects above apply to anything adapted from them; nothing here copies their text.
