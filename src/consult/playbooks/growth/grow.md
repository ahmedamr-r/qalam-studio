## What to find out

- What growing means to them as a number, and by when: more customers, more orders from each, more in each order, or more kept from each.
- Where it stands now, and how it moved over the last few months.
- Where new customers come from, and which way brings the ones who stay.
- How many come back, and when they stop.
- The one link that's weakest today: getting people in, their first purchase, coming back, or earning enough on each sale.
- What they've tried, and what happened.
- What they have to work with: people, money, time, and anything they won't do.

## What people usually miss

- Bringing in new customers while the ones they have don't come back.
- Growth that loses money on every sale.
- No starting point, so nothing they try can be judged.
- Spreading effort over every link instead of the weakest one.
- A discount that brings customers who only come for discounts.

## How to ask

- Start from the number they want and where it is now; climb to how the business earns only when the business page doesn't say.
- Walk the customer's way through the business one link at a time, and ask what happens at each.
- For someone new: plain words ("people who order again" before "retention"), one or two questions at a time, an example with each.
- When a number isn't known, ask where it lives or how it could be counted.

## What to propose

A diagnosis at the weakest link:

- The money goal on top, in their words.
- Under it, the goal at the weakest link.
- Two to four problems there, each Assumed until something backs it.
- Under each, the cheapest check, and which to run first.
