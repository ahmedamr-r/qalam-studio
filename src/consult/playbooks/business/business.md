## What to find out

Only as deep as the work in front of you needs; the business page fills in over time, never in one sitting.

- Who it serves, and who pays: the person who uses it and the person who pays aren't always the same (a family and the parent who orders, a patient and the clinic, a shop on a marketplace and its buyers). Where it serves two sides, both.
- What they get: the job it does for them, and what they used before it, which is what it really competes with.
- How it earns: walk through one real sale ("the last order: what did the customer pay, and what did you keep?"). A fee, a cut, a margin on goods, a subscription, ads.
- What it costs to run: the biggest few costs, and which grow with every sale (delivery, payment fees, discounts) and which don't.
- How customers find it: where most new customers come from today.
- The numbers they watch: the few they'd check first thing in a week, and where those numbers live.
- Where it stands: how long it's been going, whether it's growing, flat or shrinking, how it's funded, and what the people it answers to want next (a profit, a raise, a target, a date).

## What people usually miss

- The person who pays isn't the person who uses it, so they improve the wrong side.
- What each sale costs against what it brings in: a business can grow and lose more with every order.
- The money goal and its date: growth with no number and no deadline can't be checked.
- Customers who arrive from different places behave differently, and an average hides it.
- What matters depends on the stage: a young business needs proof people want it, an older one needs margin.

## How to ask

- Never as a form. Ask the next thing the work needs, inside the conversation they're having.
- A real recent example beats a description: one order, one customer, one month.
- Roughly is fine; say so, and ask where the exact figure is kept rather than pressing for it.
- Someone new to business words gets plain ones ("what you keep from each order" before "margin"); use their terms once they use them.
- Note what they say on the business page in their own words, and mark a reading of your own as yours.

## What to propose

- The money goal at the top when there isn't one: what the business wants to earn or keep, and by when, in their words.
- Under it, the goal the work in front of you moves.
- The lines noted on the business page, which they can fix or take off.
